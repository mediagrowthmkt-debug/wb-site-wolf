Room additions

Room additions & bump-outs

A room addition adds finished square footage on the same level, a bigger family room, a new bedroom, a home office or a first-floor suite. It ties into your existing foundation, roofline and walls, so the carpentry has to match the house. A bump-out is a smaller version, extending a room by a few feet, sometimes on a cantilever without a full new foundation, which makes it a cost-effective way to stretch a cramped kitchen or bathroom.

For many Peabody homes on tight lots, a well-planned room addition or bump-out adds exactly the space you need without moving.

Good candidates

  • Cramped kitchen or bath
  • Need a first-floor bedroom
  • Growing family
  • Home office space
Build up

Second-story additions

A second-story addition doubles your living space without giving up yard, which matters on the smaller lots common in Peabody. It is the biggest type of addition: we evaluate whether the existing foundation and walls can carry a new floor, reinforce where needed, and tie the new roof into the home. Because the roof comes off during construction, most families plan to be out for a few weeks. The payoff is a larger home in the neighborhood you already love, without the cost and stress of moving. We also coordinate temporary weatherproofing so your home stays protected while the new floor goes up, and we sequence the framing and roofing to keep the timeline as tight as possible.

Garage

Garage conversions & garage additions

Converting a garage into finished living space is one of the most cost-effective additions because it reuses the existing structure, no new foundation. We handle insulation, HVAC, flooring, and the egress window a bedroom requires by code. A garage makes a great home office, in-law suite, gym or extra bedroom. In-law & ADU

In-law suites & ADUs in Peabody

An in-law suite or accessory dwelling unit (ADU) is a self-contained living space with its own entrance, bathroom and often a kitchenette, ideal for aging parents, adult children or rental income. Massachusetts recently made it easier to build ADUs, with new statewide rules that allow accessory dwelling units by right in many single-family zones. That means an ADU can be a smart, now-approved way to add flexible space and value to a Peabody home. We help you plan the layout, meet code for a separate dwelling, and handle the permitting.

ADU uses

  • Aging-in-place for parents
  • Adult children
  • Rental income
  • Home office or studio

Cost

How much does a home addition cost in Peabody?

Addition pricing depends on the type, the size, whether you need a new foundation, and the finishes. As a general guide for Peabody and the North Shore:

  • Bump-out (kitchen/bath): roughly $18,000 to $45,000, no full foundation.
  • Garage conversion: roughly $25,000 to $60,000, reuses the existing structure.
  • Room addition (one room): roughly $45,000 to $110,000, foundation plus roof tie-in.
  • Sunroom: roughly $22,000 (three-season) to $80,000 (four-season with HVAC).
  • In-law suite / ADU: roughly $90,000 to $200,000, full kitchen and bath.
  • Second-story addition: $150,000 and up, structural reinforcement.

These are typical ranges, not a quote. The only way to know your real number is a walkthrough, and we give every Peabody homeowner a clear written estimate for free.

What moves the price

  • New foundation vs. reuse
  • Size & number of rooms
  • Roof & structural work
  • Kitchen or bath plumbing
  • Site access & finishes
Permits

Home addition permits & building codes in Peabody

Almost every home addition in Peabody requires a building permit from the Peabody Building Department, plus separate electrical, plumbing and mechanical permits. The work must meet the Massachusetts State Building Code (780 CMR), and additions also have to respect local zoning, setbacks and lot coverage, an addition too close to a property line can require a variance. Inspections happen at set stages, from foundation and framing to final. Wolf Carpenters handles the entire permit and inspection process for you, so you are never chasing paperwork or town approvals.

Timeline

How long does a home addition take?

Timelines depend on the size and type. A bump-out or garage conversion can take a few weeks; a full room addition usually runs 8 to 16 weeks from demolition to final walkthrough; a second-story addition or in-law suite can take several months. Design, permitting and material lead times all affect the schedule. We give you a realistic timeline up front and keep you posted at every stage.

Rough duration

  • Bump-out: 3 to 6 weeks
  • Garage conversion: 4 to 8 weeks
  • Room addition: 8 to 16 weeks
  • Second-story / ADU: 3 to 6 months

Choose well

Home addition mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping permits: unpermitted square footage can fail inspection and stall a future sale.

Yes. Additions require a building permit from the Peabody Building Department, plus electrical, plumbing and mechanical permits, and must meet the Massachusetts State Building Code and local zoning. Wolf Carpenters handles all of it for you.

How long does an addition take?
